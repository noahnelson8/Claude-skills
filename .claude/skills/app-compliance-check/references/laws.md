# Why each check matters (plain-language law notes)

**Last checked: 2026-11-24.** Laws and penalty amounts change, often yearly. Re-verify anything you quote if this date is more than six months old, and say "up to" for every figure: these are maximums, not what any business would owe. Not legal advice.

| Check | Main law(s) | What it requires, in short | Penalty scale (maximums) | Source to re-verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 Privacy policy | California CalOPPA (any site collecting info from California residents); CCPA/CPRA (larger businesses: over ~$26.6M revenue, or buying/selling/sharing data of 100k+ consumers, or 50%+ revenue from selling/sharing); other state privacy laws; GDPR for EU users | Post a policy that accurately lists data collected, purposes and who it's shared with; CCPA adds rights requests and "Do Not Sell or Share" | CCPA administrative fines about $2.5k per violation, $7.5k intentional (inflation-adjusted); breach suits $100–$750 per consumer; an inaccurate policy is also an FTC "deceptive practice" | cppa.ca.gov; oag.ca.gov/privacy |
| 4 Ad pixels / session replay | CCPA "sharing"; state wiretap laws (e.g. California CIPA) used in mass lawsuits over pixels, chat widgets and session replay | Disclose; offer opt-out of sharing; get consent where required | CIPA statutory damages claimed at $5,000 per violation in many suits | Recent CIPA case law |
| 5 Deletion | CCPA right to delete; FTC Act (if the policy promises deletion and it doesn't happen, that's deceptive) | Do what the policy says; honour deletion requests | FTC orders and penalties; CCPA fines | ftc.gov |
| 6 Public storage | State breach-notification laws; CCPA private right of action for breaches caused by unreasonable security; FTC unfair security practices | Reasonable security for personal data | CCPA breach damages $100–$750 per consumer per incident, or actual damages | oag.ca.gov |
| 7 Fake reviews / testimonials | FTC **Consumer Review and Testimonial Rule** (in force since Oct 2024); FTC Endorsement Guides | No fake or AI-generated reviews, no undisclosed paid reviews, no suppressing negative reviews, no fake social proof | Civil penalty up to **$53,088 per violation** (2025 figure; adjusted yearly) | ftc.gov "consumer reviews and testimonials" |
| 8–9 Cancel / trials / auto-renew | Federal **ROSCA** (clear terms before billing, express consent, simple cancel); FTC Act §5. The FTC's 2024 "click-to-cancel" rule was **vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025**; the FTC restarted rulemaking in 2026 (no final rule as of last check). **State auto-renewal laws** (California ARL is the strictest: online cancel, trial and renewal disclosures, reminders) | Disclose price, renewal and how to cancel before charging; affirmative consent; easy online cancel; reminders | ROSCA enforced with civil penalties and refunds; state AG and class actions | ftc.gov "negative option"; California B&P Code §17600 |
| 10 AI chatbot crisis handling | California **SB 243** companion-chatbot law (effective **Jan 1, 2026**): AI disclosure, a protocol to prevent suicide/self-harm content with referrals to crisis services, published protocol, annual reporting to the Office of Suicide Prevention from July 2027; extra protections for minors | Have and publish a crisis protocol; disclose it's AI | Private right of action: greater of actual damages or **$1,000 per violation**, plus fees | leginfo.legislature.ca.gov SB 243; check amendments (e.g. SB 1119) |
| 11 SMS consent | **TCPA** (prior express written consent for marketing texts), carrier A2P 10DLC rules, state "mini-TCPA" laws (e.g. Florida, Oklahoma) | Clear opt-in, STOP/HELP, honour opt-outs, texting hours in some states | **$500–$1,500 per message** in private suits | fcc.gov; CTIA messaging principles |
| 11 Email | **CAN-SPAM** | Unsubscribe link, physical address, honest subject and sender | Up to $53,088 per email (same FTC figure) | ftc.gov CAN-SPAM guide |
| 12 Accessibility | ADA Title III (courts apply it to websites); state laws (e.g. California Unruh Act) | Usable by people with disabilities; WCAG 2.1 AA is the usual yardstick | Unruh: $4,000 minimum per violation in California suits, plus fees | ada.gov; w3.org/WAI |
| 13 Cookies | GDPR/ePrivacy (EU), UK PECR; US state opt-out laws | Consent before non-essential cookies (EU/UK) | GDPR up to 4% of global turnover | ico.org.uk; edpb.europa.eu |
| 14 Children | **COPPA** | Verifiable parental consent before collecting data from under-13s | Up to $53,088 per violation | ftc.gov COPPA |

Sources consulted on 2026-11-24 for the items most likely to have changed: Gibson Dunn and Jones Day alerts on the FTC negative-option rulemaking (Feb and May 2026); Skadden and Bass Berry on SB 243 (Oct 2025); Venable and Benesch on the FTC Consumer Review Rule (Dec 2025).
