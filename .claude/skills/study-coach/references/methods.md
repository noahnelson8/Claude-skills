# Study methods

## Question formats by ladder level
| Level | Goal | Good formats | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Recall | Know the term | "What is …?", fill the blank, match term to definition | Trick wording |
| 2 Understand | Explain why | "Explain in your own words…", "Why does … happen?", "What would change if …?" | Yes/no questions |
| 3 Apply | Use the method | A calculation with realistic numbers; "Which formula applies here and why?" | Problems that need info not in the course |
| 4 Analyze | Interpret and compare | "Project A has higher NPV but lower IRR. Which do you pick and why?", spot the error in a worked solution, read a table/chart | Pure recall dressed up as analysis |
| 5 Evaluate/Exam | Judge a full case | Multi-step case like an exam question; defend a recommendation; short essay outline | Questions with no defensible answer |

Mix formats: multiple choice is fine for level 1–2 warm-ups, but most questions should make him produce the answer (recall beats recognition).

## Grading
- **Right:** the key idea is correct and complete enough for exam credit. Calculation: correct method and answer within rounding.
- **Partial:** right idea but a missing piece, wrong units/sign, or a correct method with an arithmetic slip.
- **Wrong:** wrong concept or method, or "I don't know".
Always give the one-line reason and the source. For partials, name exactly the missing piece.

## Feynman rounds
1. "Explain <concept> to someone who hasn't taken this class. Use an example."
2. Compare with the material: ✓ correct points · ✗ wrong or fuzzy · ○ missing.
3. Ask him to re-explain only ✗ and ○. Max 3 rounds. Record right when he can explain it cleanly without prompts.

## Spaced repetition (the tracker does the scheduling)
Leitner boxes 0–5; review intervals 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 days. Right moves a concept up a box, partial keeps it and brings it back tomorrow, wrong drops it to box 0. A short session every day beats one long cram: 15–25 minutes, 8–12 questions.

## Re-teaching a miss
3–5 sentences: the idea in plain words → the formula or rule from his materials → a tiny example with small numbers → the common mistake he just made. Then a new question on the same concept, different angle.

## Finance-specific notes (FINAN courses)
- Compute every number in Python before showing it; show cash-flow timelines as a small table (t = 0, 1, 2 …).
- State sign conventions (outflows negative) and whether rates are annual/periodic; these are the most common partial-credit losses.
- When his course uses a financial calculator or Excel functions (NPV(), IRR(), PMT()), show the equivalent keystrokes or function arguments next to the formula.
